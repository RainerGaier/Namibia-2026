
Here are the key points and recent updates on crossing at Buitepos Border Post (between Namibia and Botswana) and bringing a South African-registered motorcycle into Namibia via that or similar crossings (note: Buitepos is more commonly the Namibia-Botswana border, so if you meant the Namibia-South Africa border please check the specific post).

---
### ✅ we _must_ do

- We all need valid passports, motorcycle’s registration papers, and if not the registered owner, a letter of authority. These are general border requirements. 

- Use the correctly registered plate (South African registration), carry the ZA sticker. 

- On entry into Namibia we must obtain a **Temporary Import Permit (TIP)** for a foreign-registered vehicle (even if it’s from a SACU country). The form is provided by the Namibia Revenue Agency (or similar authority). <a href="https://www.namra.org.na/documents/cms/uploaded/temporary-import-permit--cefr060-d86b13fddb.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">(Form)</a>

- For foreign-registered vehicles (i.e. outside Namibia) including motorcycles we will need to pay any required _road user / cross-border charges_ at the border post or at the associated authority – e.g., the Road Fund Administration (RFA) in Namibia notes this for foreign-registered vehicles. <a href="https://www.rfanam.com.na/cbc-cross-border-charges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">(Road Fund Administration)</a>
 ---

### ⚠️ Specifics for Buitepos / Notes & caveats

- According to the “Namibia Handbook” (though somewhat older) the Buitepos/Mamuno post (Namibia-Botswana) lists opening hours 07:00-24:00. If you meant Buitepos as the Botswana/Namibia border, check that you are allowed to bring South Africa-registered vehicles via that route and re-export them properly.

- The RFA cross-border charges document notes that all **foreign-registered vehicles** entering Namibia must pay CBC (Cross Border Charge) and obtain the permit at the border. Buitepos is listed under “Trans-Kalahari (Buitepos)”. 

- For SACU country vehicles (i.e., South Africa vehicles) some sources say a Carnet is **not** required. E.g., a blog says: “Entering Namibia from SA or vice-versa does not require a TIP or carnet for a SADC registered vehicle.” However this needs confirmation and may differ depending on motorcycle vs car, or length of stay.

- The official Temporary Import Permit form and directive say “A temporary import permit must be produced on demand, is valid for the duration of the visit, and must be handed over upon departure.” 

---
### 🧭 What this means for our motorcycle adventure trip

- At Buitepos plan to stop at customs/immigration, and at the RFA (or relevant Namibia road authority) to pay any CBC/road-user charge and get the permit.

- When entering Namibia, carry all vehicle documents, pay the required fees, obtain the permit, and keep it with the motorcycle.

- On exiting Namibia, ensure we hand back the temporary import permits (or have it stamped) to show we have re-exported the vehicle correctly.

- Even though some sources say SACU-registered vehicles may not need a carnet, we should double-check with the local border post and confirm for motorcycles, especially if we intend to ride via remote areas (like conservancies) and return to South Africa.

- Because Buitepos is a border between Namibia and Botswana, our route starts in South Africa, we will cross into Botswana first then Namibia — we need to ensure each border crossing is compliant.

- Check the fee scale: e.g., older handbook shows for motorcycles/quadricycles the entry charge: “R 188 for motorcycles …” when entering Namibia. 

---
### 🔍 Recommendation

Before we depart, contact the Buitepos Border Post (Namibia side) or the nearest Namibian customs office to confirm:

1. That the permit/temporary import process for a South African-registered motorcycle is the same as for cars.
2. The exact fee amount for a motorcycle for your length of stay.
3. Any special conditions for remote travel (into conservancies, etc).
4. The opening hours and whether they can handle registration/permits for motorcycles.

## Fee schedule

Here are the **latest published fee schedules** and relevant details for vehicle cross-border charges (CBC) and related permits for foreign-registered vehicles entering Road Fund Administration (RFA) jurisdiction in Namibia (including via the Buitepos Border Post).

---

### ✅ Key published fees

- The RFA’s official “Charges – Cross-Border Charges (Entry Fees) for Foreign-Registered Vehicles (Effective 1 May 2024)” list shows:

    - For **motorcycles, motor tricycles, motor quadracycles, caravans & light trailers drawn by Type 2 vehicles**: **NAD 283**. 
    - For **motor cars, single and double cab goods vehicles (bakkies), 2x4 and 4x4 goods vehicles**: **NAD 445**. 
    - For heavier categories there are higher fees (light goods vehicles, heavy trucks etc). 

- The RFA site confirms that the CBC (Cross Border Charge) must be paid by foreign-registered vehicles at the border post RFA office. 

- A travel information site states for **vehicles entering Namibia**: “The CBC **costs N$445 (as of March 2025)** and is valid for three months.” 


---

### ⚠️ Interpretation & practical implications for you (motorcycle from South Africa)

- Since your case is a motorcycle registered in South Africa entering Namibia and then returning: the published fee of **NAD 283** (as of 1 May 2024) applies to motorcycles/ tricycles etc.

- The fee is valid at least until updated and must be paid at the relevant border post RFA office (e.g., Buitepos for the Trans-Kalahari route) when you enter.

- You’ll need to carry the permit/certificate issued after payment in the vehicle at all times. Non-compliance may incur fines (e.g., up to NAD 4,000 for missing permit) according to RFA. 

- Check validity: some sources say the permit (CBC) may be valid for up to three months. 

- Because your vehicle is South African-registered (a SACU country), although some blogs suggest fewer burdens, the official fee schedule above still appears to apply for “foreign-registered vehicles” (i.e., non-Namibian). So plan on paying.

- At Buitepos (Trans-Kalahari route) there is an RFA CBC office as listed on RFA’s site. 


---

### ✅ What you should do

- On entering Namibia via Buitepos, stop at the RFA CBC office, declare your motorcycle, pay the NAD 283 fee (confirm current rate there), get the permit/certificate.

- Keep the permit with you on the bike throughout your stay.

- On exiting Namibia, present the permit to RFA/Border post to show proper re-exportation of the vehicle and compliance.

- Check with the border post just before your trip to confirm if there have been any updates or changes to the fee (since fees can change).

- Ensure your other vehicle/driver documents are in order (registration papers, ZA sticker, insurance, letter of authority if needed, etc).
