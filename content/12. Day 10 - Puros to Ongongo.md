
>
>A longer day in the saddle (192kms), typically described as a **slow, technical 4×4 day** rather than a “road drive”.
>We start the day riding through the beautiful **Puros canyon**. 
>One will spend long sections in **dry riverbeds** (sand ranging from firm to very soft), with frequent in/out of the river course depending on braiding and obstacles. 
>Typically why trip times vary wildly.
>
>**Permits / gates and controlled access**
>There is a mention of a **permit gate / fee** when entering the Hoanib riverbed trail system near Sesfontein (cash payment), where travellers are briefed on rules. 
>Exact prices change, but the existence of a gate/permit process appears to be a consistent theme.
>
>**Wildlife risk**
>The Hoanib / Puros corridors are specifically known for **desert-adapted elephants**, and travellers explicitly warn you can “come across elephants at any time” in the river systems.
>Recommended to stay as a group.
>

### Hoanib River 

 >
 >The Hoanib river is one of the 12 ephemeral seasonal rivers in the west of Namibia, and acts as a border between northern Damaraland and Kaokoland. 
 >Measuring 270km’s in length and considering its very low population density it is considered a veritable oasis to the wildlife inhabiting this region. 
 >When dry it teems with large groups of wildlife, all finding respite from the intense day time heat reaching up to 42 degrees celsius. 
 >The riverbed and its giant Ana trees and Leadwood offer large tracts of shade to Oryx, elephant, giraffe **and a few lion prides**. 
 >The terrain is incredible, the valley being one of the last true wilderness areas in Namibia. 
 >Canyons created in weathered stone by strong winds and water erosion bend and twist their way through the landscape, and it is almost inconceivable that during occasional floods the water levels can be several metres high and last for a few days. 
 >Some years the water makes its way to the mouth in the Atlantic, whilst most of it will seep into the soil and become part of the subsurface aquifer. 
 >
